The Construction

The Details

Nothing here is accidental. Every opening, every edge and every gram of metal is a decision — made once, and made to last.

Premium cotton 01

Long-staple cotton, chosen for the length of its fibre rather than its softness alone. It resists pilling and holds its shape through years of wear.

Aged ivory 02

A bone tone developed, not dyed. The colour shifts subtly with light and laundering, much as paper does over time.

Hand-like openings 03

Irregular apertures are opened by hand so that no two garments are identical. Precision lives in the control, not the symmetry.

Burned & torn edges 04

Edges are torn along the grain and sealed with a controlled heat. The result is finished, intentional, and quietly imperfect.

Gold chain 05

A fine antique-gold chain, weighted to sit still against the chest. It is the single moment of metal the piece permits itself.

Leather detail 06

Vegetable-tanned leather, hand-stitched and burnished at the edge. It will darken and soften into a personal patina.

The Idea

Heritage, carried forward — not preserved in glass.

This garment began with a question about inheritance: what do we carry, and what are we willing to let change? Its material is traditional. Its construction is deliberately not.

The openings are not damage. They are the point at which the past is allowed in. A piece that keeps everything intact keeps everything at a distance; this one is made to be worn, altered by the body, and lived in.

It should read as though it carries a history — and remain, entirely, of this moment.

Provenance

An inheritance, kept quiet.

ADISS was founded between Addis Ababa and the Gulf, and that duality lives in the work rather than on it. There are no borrowed motifs here and no signals worn on the sleeve.

What remains is a standard: patience with material, respect for the hand, and the belief that the most valuable things are understood slowly. Heritage, in this house, is a method — never a decoration.

“The loom is older than the trend.”
